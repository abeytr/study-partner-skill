## Marking conventions

History is the most point-count-strict of the four subjects:

| Marks | Expected structure |
|---|---|
| 1 | One precise fact (a name, date, event, or single cause) |
| 2 | Two distinct points OR statement + supporting fact/example |
| 3 | Three distinct points, each elaborated briefly |
| 5 | Structured paragraph: causes / course / consequences OR introduction + body + conclusion; expect 5+ points organized into coherent flow |

Source-based questions appear regularly in ICSE History — a photograph, political cartoon, or text extract is provided, and questions probe identification, context, and significance. Always start a source-based answer by identifying *what* the source is and *when* it's from before answering the substantive question.

## Answer structure templates

### Standard descriptive answers — the "C-C-C-S" frame

Most History answers fit this pattern:

1. **Causes** — what led to the event (political, economic, social factors)
2. **Course** — what happened, in chronological order, with names and dates
3. **Consequences** — what changed as a result
4. **Significance** — long-term importance, why this event matters historically

For 3-mark questions, often only one or two of these four headings is asked. For 5-mark questions, all four are expected at varying depth.

### Source-based question answers

Pattern:

1. Identify the source (what does the photograph/cartoon/extract show; what year or period)
2. Identify key figures, symbols, or phrases
3. Explain the historical context (what was happening at the time)
4. Connect to the specific event or movement the question asks about

### Dates, names, and proper nouns matter literally

ICSE History marking is sensitive to specifics. The closing-protocol marking must flag missing specifics.

- "Around 1920" is weaker than "1920"
- "Gandhi launched the movement" is weaker than "Mahatma Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement at the Nagpur Session of the INC in December 1920"
- "Hitler invaded Poland" is weaker than "On 1 September 1939, Hitler's Germany invaded Poland, triggering Britain and France's declaration of war"

Marking should explicitly call out when a vague phrasing could be replaced by a specific one.

## Common confusables — narrower than Biology, but real

- **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) vs Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34)** — different methods (boycott of British titles, schools, courts vs active breaking of unjust laws starting with the Salt March), different triggers (Khilafat issue + Jallianwala Bagh + Rowlatt Act vs Simon Commission + failure of Round Table negotiations + Salt Tax), different ends (suspended after Chauri Chaura violence vs Gandhi-Irwin Pact).
- **Lucknow Pact (1916) vs Poona Pact (1932)** — Congress-Muslim League agreement on joint constitutional demands vs Gandhi-Ambedkar agreement reserving seats for Dalits within Hindu electorate.
- **INA vs Azad Hind Fauj vs Forward Bloc** — Indian National Army (the military force under Bose, 1942 onwards) vs the Hindi name for the same INA vs the political party Subhas Bose founded in 1939 inside the Congress before leaving for armed struggle.
- **League of Nations vs United Nations** — League formed 1920 after WWI, headquartered in Geneva, failed to prevent WWII vs UN formed 1945 after WWII, headquartered in New York, includes Security Council with five permanent veto-holding members and specialised agencies.
- **Treaty of Versailles vs Treaty of Saint-Germain vs Treaty of Trianon** — with Germany vs with Austria vs with Hungary, all part of the post-WWI settlement.
- **Nazism vs Fascism** — Germany under Hitler vs Italy under Mussolini; broadly similar in authoritarianism and ultranationalism, but Nazism added an explicit racial ideology (Aryan supremacy, antisemitism, Lebensraum doctrine) that Italian Fascism did not centre.

## Productive wrongness patterns for History

- **Wrong date by a year or two**: "Quit India was launched in 1944" (it was 1942).
- **Wrong leader for an event**: "Tilak led the Non-Cooperation Movement" (Gandhi did; Tilak died in 1920 just before it began).
- **Conflate two movements**: blur Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience, or merge their causes.
- **Wrong location**: subtle ones — "the INC was founded in Calcutta" (it was Bombay, December 1885).
- **Miss a consequence**: list two consequences when the question wants four or five.
- **Use vague language where specifics matter**: "many people died" instead of naming numbers or groups.
- **Mix up treaties or agreements**: claim the Treaty of Versailles dealt with Austria.

## Mode-specific guidance

**Elaboration mode** — this is History's primary mode. Most questions are 3-mark or 5-mark descriptive. The closing-protocol marking should explicitly check: (1) was the C-C-C-S structure followed? (2) Are dates, leaders, and proper nouns precise? (3) Is the point count matched to the mark value? (4) Is there an opening line that sets context?

**Discrimination mode** — narrower use; reserve for the genuine confusables above. Don't manufacture History wrongness — the subject's confusables are specific. Most fertile pairs: Non-Cooperation vs Civil Disobedience; League of Nations vs UN; the various post-WWI treaties.

**Spaced retrieval mode** — dates and proper nouns are the highest-decay items. Revisit them frequently. The "10 dates the student keeps mixing up" makes a natural recurring revision set.
