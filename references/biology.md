# ICSE Class 10 Biology — Conventions for the Study Partner

Read this before running a Biology session. This is the subject where Discrimination mode pays the highest dividends.

## Syllabus areas (ICSE Class 10)

- Cell structure, cell cycle and division (mitosis, meiosis)
- Genetics (basic Mendelian principles, sex determination)
- Absorption by roots, transpiration
- Photosynthesis and respiration
- Circulatory system (heart, blood, blood vessels, lymph)
- Excretory system (kidney, nephron)
- Nervous system (neuron, brain, spinal cord, reflex arc)
- Endocrine system (glands and their hormones)
- Reproduction (male and female systems, menstrual cycle, fertilisation)
- Population (basic concepts)
- Pollution and ecology

## Marking conventions

| Marks | Expected structure |
|---|---|
| 1 | One precise definition, term, or labelled part |
| 2 | Definition + function OR two distinct points |
| 3 | Three distinct points OR definition + function + significance OR a properly labelled diagram |
| 5 | Definition + structure + function + diagram, OR causes/mechanism/effects/significance for a process |

Diagrams carry significant weight in ICSE Biology — a properly labelled diagram with 4+ correct labels typically earns 2 marks on its own. Missing labels cost marks even if the drawing is correct.

## Answer structure templates

### Concept or term answers

Definition → structural description → function → significance.

### Process answers (photosynthesis, respiration, transpiration, etc.)

Definition → site/conditions → mechanism, step-by-step → equation if applicable → significance.

### Comparative answers ("differentiate between mitosis and meiosis")

Use a clear two-column structure. Number the contrast points (1, 2, 3...). Each row should contrast the *same* property — don't mix categories. Aim for 4–6 contrast rows for 5-mark questions.

### Diagrams — text-described protocol

The student names:

1. The structure being drawn
2. Each labelled part, in order (usually clockwise or top-to-bottom)
3. The function of each part
4. The arrows or flows (direction of blood flow through heart; direction of filtrate through nephron; etc.)

Diagrams the student should master:

- Heart (chambers, valves, vessels, pulmonary and systemic circulation)
- Nephron (Bowman's capsule, PCT, loop of Henle, DCT, collecting duct)
- Neuron (dendrites, cell body, axon, myelin sheath, synaptic terminal)
- Reflex arc (receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron in spinal cord → motor neuron → effector)
- Plant cell and animal cell (cell wall, plasma membrane, nucleus, cytoplasm, organelles, large vacuole only in plant cells, etc.)
- Transverse section of stem/root showing xylem and phloem arrangement

## Common confusables — Discrimination mode's home territory

Biology has the richest confusable list of the four subjects. Discrimination mode should run heavily here.

- **Xylem vs phloem** — water and minerals up vs food (sucrose) down; dead conducting tissue vs living conducting tissue.
- **Arteries vs veins** — away from heart vs toward heart; thick muscular wall, no valves (except aortic/pulmonary) vs thin wall with valves; oxygenated vs deoxygenated EXCEPT pulmonary artery and pulmonary vein, which reverse the pattern.
- **Mitosis vs meiosis** — somatic cell division → 2 genetically identical cells vs gamete formation → 4 genetically varied cells; one division vs two divisions; produces growth/repair vs produces reproductive cells.
- **Transpiration vs translocation** — loss of water vapour from leaves through stomata vs transport of food (sucrose) through phloem.
- **Photosynthesis vs respiration** — anabolic, light-dependent, in chloroplasts vs catabolic, continuous, in mitochondria; absorbs CO₂ releases O₂ vs absorbs O₂ releases CO₂.
- **Aerobic vs anaerobic respiration** — with O₂, complete glucose breakdown to CO₂ + H₂O + 38 ATP vs without O₂, partial breakdown to lactic acid (muscles) or ethanol + CO₂ (yeast) + 2 ATP only.
- **Osmosis vs diffusion** — movement of solvent across a semipermeable membrane down its concentration gradient vs free movement of any particle (gas/solute) down its concentration gradient.
- **Hyperopia vs myopia** — long-sight, image forms behind retina, convex (converging) lens corrects vs short-sight, image forms in front of retina, concave (diverging) lens corrects.
- **Hypoxia vs asphyxiation** — reduced oxygen supply to tissues (still some oxygen) vs complete oxygen deprivation leading to suffocation.
- **Autosomes vs sex chromosomes** — 22 pairs not involved in sex determination vs 1 pair (XX female, XY male) determining biological sex.
- **Homologous vs analogous organs** — same anatomical origin, possibly different function (whale flipper / human arm) vs different origin, similar function (bird wing / butterfly wing); evidence of divergent vs convergent evolution.
- **Inhalation vs exhalation** — active in quiet breathing: diaphragm contracts/flattens, external intercostals lift ribs, thoracic volume up, pressure down, air in vs passive: diaphragm relaxes/domes up, ribs lower, thoracic volume down, pressure up, air out.

## Productive wrongness patterns for Biology

When running Discrimination mode, use shapes like:

- **Swap a confusable**: claim phloem carries water (it's xylem); say arteries always carry oxygenated blood (the pulmonary artery doesn't).
- **Conflate processes**: describe photosynthesis as happening continuously day and night (it needs light); confuse the products of photosynthesis with reactants.
- **Wrong location**: place the nephron in the liver, or cell walls in animal cells.
- **Wrong direction**: state that in transpiration, water moves downward from leaves to roots.
- **Miss a qualification**: define respiration as "breathing" (it's cellular energy production); leave out that aerobic respiration requires molecular oxygen.
- **Confuse mitosis details with meiosis** or vice versa: claim mitosis produces gametes; claim meiosis happens in skin cells.

## Mode-specific guidance

**Elaboration mode** — questions often have structured marking. Always state the mark value to the student at the start of each question; they should match the point count.

**Discrimination mode** — this is Biology's natural home. Use the confusables list aggressively. Always open with the debate contract: "Let me state my understanding — push back if you see anything off."

**Spaced retrieval mode** — confusables degrade fast under disuse. If the log shows low confidence on any confusable pair, revisit within 3 days. Diagrams (especially heart, nephron, neuron) should resurface every 1–2 weeks.
