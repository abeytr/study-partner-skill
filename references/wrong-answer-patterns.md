# Productive Wrongness — Patterns for Discrimination Mode

The Discrimination mode's value depends entirely on the AI providing the *right kind* of wrong answer. Random wrongness — fabricating values, inventing terms, claiming nonsense — wastes the student's time and risks embedding confusion. Productive wrongness, by contrast, surfaces specific confusions the student is realistically likely to have, and forces them to articulate the correct distinction.

This file catalogues the patterns to use, with examples drawn from all four subjects.

## Core principle

**Wrongness should look plausible to a Class 10 student studying this topic.**

If a student couldn't reasonably be tempted by the wrong answer, it teaches nothing — they'll dismiss it immediately and move on. If the wrong answer maps onto a real confusion that students at this level actually make, the debate cements the right understanding.

## The five productive wrongness patterns

### Pattern 1: Near-neighbour swap

Swap one term for its commonly confused partner.

- **Biology** — "Phloem carries water and minerals up from the roots." (It's xylem; phloem carries food *down*.)
- **Physics** — "A concave lens converges parallel rays to a focal point." (It's a convex lens; concave lens diverges.)
- **Geography** — "Rice is a rabi crop sown in winter." (It's a kharif crop sown in the monsoon.)
- **History** — "The Non-Cooperation Movement began in 1930." (That was Civil Disobedience; Non-Cooperation began in 1920.)

This is the most common productive wrongness pattern because confusable pairs are exactly where students lose marks on exam day.

### Pattern 2: Shallow framing (incompleteness)

Give a too-short answer that misses qualifiers or skips key structured points.

- **Biology** — "Photosynthesis is plants making food." (Missing: location in chloroplasts, light dependence, reactants CO₂ and H₂O, products glucose and O₂, energy source — sunlight, equation.)
- **Physics** — "Ohm's law says V = IR." (Missing: the conditions — constant temperature, ohmic conductor.)
- **History** — "The First World War started because Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated." (Missing: the deeper causes — alliance systems, militarism, imperialism, nationalism. The assassination was the trigger, not the cause.)
- **Geography** — "Black soil is good for cotton." (Missing: why — moisture retention, lime content, distribution across the Deccan plateau, weaknesses like poor drainage.)

This pattern is also useful in Elaboration mode, because it mirrors how students actually under-answer in exams.

### Pattern 3: Wrong qualifier or condition

Get the substance right but apply it in the wrong context or under the wrong conditions.

- **Physics** — "v = u + at applies whenever you need to find final velocity." (Only when time is known. If you have distance instead, you need v² = u² + 2as.)
- **Biology** — "Anaerobic respiration produces lactic acid." (True in muscles, but in yeast it produces ethanol and CO₂. Same right answer applied to the wrong organism.)
- **Geography** — "Laterite soil is found mainly in the northern plains." (Wrong region — it's the Western Ghats and northeast.)
- **History** — "The Treaty of Versailles dealt with Austria." (No — Versailles was with Germany. Saint-Germain was with Austria.)

### Pattern 4: Wrong direction or sign

Reverse the direction of a process or get a sign convention wrong.

- **Biology** — "In transpiration, water moves from leaves down to the roots." (Reversed — water moves from roots up to leaves, then evaporates from leaf surfaces.)
- **Physics** — "The focal length of a concave lens is positive by convention." (It's negative.)
- **Geography** — "The southwest monsoon blows from land toward the sea." (No — sea to land. That's why it carries moisture inland and brings rain.)

### Pattern 5: Conflate two related things

Mix up two related processes, events, or concepts in subtle ways.

- **Biology** — "Mitosis produces four genetically varied cells from one parent cell." (Conflates mitosis with meiosis. Mitosis produces two genetically identical cells; meiosis produces four varied gametes.)
- **Physics** — "Heat and temperature are the same thing, just in different units." (Distinct: heat is energy transferred, temperature is the measure of average kinetic energy of particles.)
- **History** — "Gandhi led the Forward Bloc against the British." (Conflates Gandhi with Bose. Forward Bloc was Bose's political party.)

## What productive wrongness is NOT

Avoid these — they teach nothing or actively confuse:

- **Random fabrication** — "The square of the hypotenuse equals the cube of the adjacent side." A Class 10 student won't be tempted by this; they'll dismiss it without engaging.
- **Off-syllabus content** — Don't construct wrongness from material the student hasn't studied. The debate should engage what they actually know.
- **Unfalsifiable wrongness** — "I think photosynthesis is a complicated topic." Vague claims don't trigger correction.
- **Wrong-but-irrelevant** — "Mahatma Gandhi was born in 1869." Even if true, if the question is about Civil Disobedience this doesn't move the debate forward.
- **Multiple wrong things at once** — Pile on one wrongness per turn. Multiple errors in one answer overwhelm rather than focus.

## The debate contract — always announce wrongness up front

Before delivering any wrong answer in Discrimination mode, announce the contract:

- "Let me state my position — push back if you see anything off."
- "I'll take a side here. Argue with me if you disagree."
- "Here's my best understanding. Tell me where you disagree."

This is non-negotiable. The student must always know that wrongness is incoming, so they engage critically rather than absorbing it as truth. Without the contract, Discrimination mode breaks — it becomes the AI silently teaching false things, which is the failure mode the skill is designed to avoid.

## The resolution rule — every Discrimination question ends with correctness

After 1–2 exchanges of debate, the skill converges with the correct understanding. The closing protocol then runs as usual — student writes the final exam-quality answer; AI marks it; AI shows the model answer last.

The correct concept must be the last thing said in the question. The misconception never gets the last word.

## Calibration — read what the student catches

If the student catches the wrong answer immediately and corrects it in one shot:

- The topic is solid. Note high confidence in the log.
- Move to a harder confusable, or to a different topic.

If the student doesn't catch the wrong answer:

- The topic is genuinely weak. Note low confidence in the log.
- Don't just deliver the correction — walk through *why* the wrong answer was wrong, what features distinguish the right one. Make the distinction explicit.
- Schedule for spaced retrieval within 2–3 days.

The skill's job is to find the edge of what the student knows, not to humiliate or to coast on easy questions.
