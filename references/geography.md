# ICSE Class 10 Geography — Conventions for the Study Partner

(HCG Paper 2 — Geography)

Read this before running a Geography session.

## Syllabus areas (ICSE Class 10)

- Location, extent, physical features of India
- Climate of India (monsoons, factors affecting climate, distribution of rainfall)
- Soils of India (alluvial, black, red, laterite, mountain, desert)
- Natural vegetation (evergreen, deciduous, thorn and scrub, mangrove, mountain)
- Water resources (rivers, dams, irrigation, ground water)
- Mineral resources (iron, coal, bauxite, petroleum, mica, manganese, copper)
- Agriculture (kharif, rabi, zaid seasons; major crops — rice, wheat, cotton, sugarcane, tea, coffee, jute)
- Industries (iron and steel, cotton textile, sugar, electronics, petrochemical)
- Transport, communication, and trade
- Waste management (sources, effects, methods of disposal)

**Map work is a substantial component** (roughly 10–15 marks of the 80-mark paper). Students must mark physical features, climatic regions, mineral belts, industrial centres, and major cities on a blank outline map of India.

## Marking conventions

| Marks | Expected structure |
|---|---|
| 1 | One precise fact (a location, name, type, or single value) |
| 2 | Two-part answer OR one with reasoning |
| 3 | Three distinct points; comparison tables roughly one mark per row |
| 5 | Structured with multiple points; often includes a labelled sketch map or comparative table |

Map work answers: each correctly identified or marked location ≈ 1 mark. Wrong location = 0 — no partial credit for being close.

## Answer structure templates

### Descriptive answers

Definition or type → distribution (where in India) → characteristics → uses or significance → (sometimes) limitations.

Example for "Describe black soil" (5 marks):

1. Type and origin — volcanic, formed by weathering of basalt lava
2. Distribution — Deccan plateau covering Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, parts of Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh
3. Characteristics — clayey, retains moisture, develops cracks in dry weather, dark colour from iron compounds, rich in lime, iron, magnesium, alumina
4. Suitable crops — cotton (hence "black cotton soil"), sugarcane, tobacco, oilseeds
5. Limitations — poor drainage, sticky and hard to work when wet, develops deep cracks when dry

### Comparative answers ("differentiate between kharif and rabi crops")

Use a two-column or three-column table. One row per contrasted property. Don't mix property categories across rows. Aim for 4–6 rows for 5-mark questions.

### Map work — text-described protocol

For map questions, the student should list each item with:

- Name
- State or surrounding region for context
- Type (mineral belt / climatic region / river / industry / port / peak)
- Approximate position description ("northeast of Mumbai", "in the Western Ghats", "on the Coromandel coast")

Map items the student should master:

- Major rivers: Ganga, Brahmaputra, Krishna, Godavari, Narmada, Tapi, Mahanadi, Kaveri, Indus (with main tributaries and the states they pass through)
- Peaks: Anaimudi (south), Kanchenjunga (east), Nanda Devi, K2 (extreme north)
- Iron ore belts: Jharkhand-Odisha, Karnataka (Bellary-Hospet), Goa
- Coal fields: Jharia (Jharkhand), Raniganj (West Bengal), Korba (Chhattisgarh), Singareni (Telangana)
- Bauxite areas: Odisha, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh
- Major ports: Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Cochin (Kochi), Visakhapatnam, Kandla, Mormugao, Tuticorin
- Industrial centres: Jamshedpur (Jharkhand), Bhilai (Chhattisgarh), Durgapur (West Bengal), Rourkela (Odisha), Bokaro (Jharkhand), Vishakhapatnam (Andhra Pradesh)
- Climatic regions: hot desert (Rajasthan), monsoon, tropical wet (Western Ghats and northeast), tropical wet-dry, mountain

## Common confusables — Discrimination mode targets

- **Kharif vs rabi crops** — sown June–July, harvested October–November (rice, maize, cotton, jute, groundnut, soybean, bajra, jowar) vs sown October–November, harvested March–April (wheat, barley, gram, mustard, peas). Zaid is the short summer season between rabi and kharif (watermelon, cucumber, fodder crops).
- **Khadar vs bhangar alluvial soil** — new alluvium, near rivers, very fertile, renewed by annual flooding vs old alluvium, away from current river course, contains kankar (calcium carbonate) nodules, less fertile.
- **Red vs laterite soil** — formed from crystalline igneous rocks (granite, gneiss), found mostly across southern Deccan, iron-rich (red colour from iron oxide) vs formed by leaching in tropical wet-and-dry climate, found in Western Ghats and northeastern hills, also used for building bricks.
- **Black vs alluvial soil** — clayey, moisture-retentive, formed from basalt, Deccan plateau, ideal for cotton vs sandy-silty, river-deposited, found in the northern Indo-Gangetic plain and coastal deltas, supports most Indian agriculture by area.
- **Evergreen vs deciduous forests** — heavy rainfall (>200 cm), Western Ghats and northeastern India, trees retain leaves year-round (rosewood, mahogany, ebony) vs moderate rainfall (70–200 cm), most of India, shed leaves in dry season (teak, sal, sandalwood).
- **Monsoon winds vs trade winds** — seasonal reversal of wind direction over land/sea, defining feature of South Asian climate vs steady winds blowing toward the equator from the subtropical high-pressure belts (northeast and southeast trades).
- **Renewable vs non-renewable resources** — replenished on a human timescale (solar, wind, flowing water, forests if managed) vs finite, exhaustible (coal, petroleum, most minerals).
- **Large-scale vs small-scale industries** — heavy capital investment, employs hundreds or thousands (iron and steel, cotton mills, automobile) vs lower capital, labour-intensive, often household-based (cottage industries, handloom, handicrafts).

## Productive wrongness patterns for Geography

- **Swap a soil type's characteristics**: claim laterite is dark and clayey (no — black soil is); say red soil is highly fertile (it's not — needs fertilisers).
- **Wrong season for a crop**: place rice as a rabi crop (it's kharif); place wheat as kharif (it's rabi).
- **Wrong location for an industry**: place Jamshedpur in Karnataka (it's Jharkhand); place Bhilai in Maharashtra (it's Chhattisgarh).
- **Wrong river basin**: claim the Krishna flows through Odisha (the Mahanadi does, mostly); claim the Godavari rises in the Himalayas (it rises in the Western Ghats).
- **Miss a defining characteristic**: define laterite without mentioning leaching, or black soil without mentioning moisture retention.
- **Wrong monsoon direction**: state that the southwest monsoon blows from land to sea (it's sea to land; that's why it brings rain).

## Mode-specific guidance

**Elaboration mode** — primary mode. Use comparison tables aggressively for "differentiate between" questions, which appear frequently. Always state the mark value so the student knows how many points to produce.

**Discrimination mode** — use the soil/crop/forest/season confusables list. Geography has fewer confusable pairs than Biology, but the ones it has are reliable.

**Map work** — handled as text-described retrieval. Don't try to draw or describe maps visually; ask the student to recite locations with state and approximate position.

**Spaced retrieval mode** — locations, state-region pairings, and specific industry-location facts degrade quickly. Revisit map items frequently — every 1–2 weeks for the core list above.
